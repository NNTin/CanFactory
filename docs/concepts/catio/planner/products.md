### Generic hardware-store specifications that must remain available

- Timber: species/grade or other recipe requirement, planed finished section, required stock length, suitable use/exposure, number of stock lengths and cuts; distinguish a cut member from the stock it consumes.
- Sheet: material, thickness, actual sheet width/length, exposure requirements, cut list, grain/face direction, and edge finish.
- Mesh/net: opening size, wire/cord type and diameter where published, usable roll width, purchased length, panel cuts, seam/edge support and fixing method.
- Rope: material, diameter, actual sold length, purpose (scratch covering versus structural support), termination allowances and replacement guidance.
- Fasteners: material/coating, thread/diameter, length, head, compatible washers/nuts/catches, quantity and purchased pack size.
- Seals/pads: material/profile, thickness, width, required lengths and join allowances.
- Fabric: composition/use, thickness/weight where needed, usable width, cut size, hem/seam allowance, reinforcing material and attachment construction.
- Finishes/adhesives: specified use, cure requirements, applicable manufacturer evidence for the intended exposure/contact; do not label a generic product “pet-safe” without evidence.
- Tools: bought/owned separately from consumable material; do not multiply the tool count by number of components.

## Quantity examples for acceptance tests

These examples define calculation behavior; they are not load-bearing construction recipes.

### Sisal wrapping

Let a circular core diameter be D, square-core side a, rope diameter d, and wrapped height H. Units are millimetres.

- Circular rope centreline perimeter: P = π(D + d).
- Square rope centreline perimeter approximation with rounded rope turns: P = 4a + πd.
- Turns: n = ceil(H / d).
- Approximate wound length: n × sqrt(P² + d²).
- Add the recipe's start/end termination allowance, then the visible waste allowance (default 10% for rope), then round up to purchasable roll lengths.

For a 90 mm square core, 1000 mm wrapped height and 8 mm rope, with an illustrative total 200 mm termination allowance: approximately **53.19 m including 10% allowance**, so **two 30 m rolls**. The UI must describe this as an estimate; real winding/compression and termination details can change consumption. Use actual rope diameter, not an unacknowledged midpoint of P02's range.

### Linear cutting

Two 1200 mm finished members do not fit one 2400 mm stock member when a separating saw cut consumes 3 mm. Do not round away the kerf or silently shorten either finished member.

### Sheet fitting

A 1300 × 1300 mm unspliced panel does not fit a 2500 × 1250 mm sheet in either orientation, even though its area is smaller. Report the need for different stock or an explicitly supported joint recipe.

### Pack aggregation

If two assemblies each require 12 of the same fastener and it is sold in packs of 10, the project needs 24 fasteners / 3 packs, not two independently rounded component purchases totalling 4 packs. Show 6 spare.

### Mesh quantities

Use the actual selected roll width and a feasible panel cutting layout. A 2 m panel does not fit a 1.01 m roll as one piece. Any split/overlap must be represented by an explicit supported seam and its extra battens/fasteners, rather than inferred from total area.

## Verification required before enabling a construction recipe

1. Record the exact stock/parts and dimensional ranges the recipe supports.
2. Derive every cut and count from the same assembly model used by the scene.
3. Resolve all mounting, weather exposure, clearance, seam and attachment details.
4. Document the evidence for any supported span/load and physical tests needed for new printed or custom joints; do not borrow an isolated fitting rating for the assembly.
5. Provide assembly steps, tools, inspection/maintenance and replacement access.
6. Only mark the resulting pack complete when every required item and unresolved check is accounted for.
